EXPLORATORY RELEASE · OCTOBER 2026

Methods and key choices

This account distinguishes completed collection and coding from proposed validation. The release contains 2,846 occupation-ad observations: 346 UX research, 500 design, 500 product management, 500 marketing, and 1,000 software engineering. It contains 99 operational competencies. Automated matches are retrieval signals. Contextual AI review is not independent human validation.

Motivation and research questions

AI may change both the tools people use and the activities their professions take responsibility for. A generic “AI skills” label hides those differences. A researcher using AI to synthesize interviews is doing something different from researching trust in an AI product. A product manager building an agent for customers is doing something different from creating an agent to support their own product work.

This project asks which specific activities employers describe, how these differ and overlap across occupations, and which emerging capabilities appear rarely. It is intended to support discussion among practitioners, educators and students. Job advertisements provide evidence of advertised expectations. They do not directly measure workplace adoption, worker proficiency, productivity or job displacement.

The starting inspiration was Andrew Ng’s AI Engineering Skills Map, discussed at BayLearn. Ng describes a synthesis of more than 10,000 job postings, structured interviews, surveys and other material. He explicitly presents AI engineering as a set of skills relevant to developers broadly, rather than only people with an “AI Engineer” job title. We therefore sample software engineering without requiring an AI title. Our engineering hierarchy follows his published four branches and 20 second-level categories; our operational rules, sample and numerical results are our own. This is not a replication of his sampling procedure or frequency overlay.

Reading the skill percentages

Each card’s percentage is its recorded ad count divided by the entire occupation sample. All bars share a 0–100% scale and a fixed six-pixel height. UXR, design and PM show the share with AI-reviewed support recorded so far, while marketing retains its earlier full-ad AI-assisted review and software engineering shows automated matches. The candidate-selected review subset is never the denominator. The unfilled portion includes ads without recorded support, unreviewed text and inconclusive evidence; it does not establish absence. These percentages are descriptive coding results, not validated prevalence or statistical lower bounds. Independent review may change the results.

How the project developed

  1. Feasibility and scope. Early small, varied examples helped identify useful distinctions, but could not support frequency claims. The scope separated UX research, digital product design and product management because their tasks and denominators differ. The target expanded from small examples to 100 and then 500 ads per occupation.
  2. Institutional source test. SCU library access to Reference Solutions / My Jobs Library supported a first 100-ad pilot for each of the three occupations. The team tested search enumeration and full-ad access. These first pilot results remain a separate source and coding round.
  3. Public-source expansion. OpenJobData made a broader, inspectable frame available. A minimal-record feasibility draw was followed by full-description recovery. Blank or short minimal descriptions were not treated as negative AI evidence.
  4. Recency and archived ads. The main source was pinned to an October 16, 2026 revision. Closed ads were included to enlarge the frame; known original posting dates were limited to 2026, with unknown dates retained separately. The user chose this 2026 scope with recency comparisons rather than historical 2024 ads.
  5. From traditional task maps to emerging competencies. Initial maps placed AI evidence within familiar occupational activities. A separate frontier map then made emerging activities explicit, including synthetic-user research, AI-assisted prototypes, research agents, agent interaction design and AI product evaluation. The traditional maps were retained locally.
  6. Operational definitions and calibration. Terminology was revised toward established practitioner terms. Categories gained inclusion/exclusion rules, participation levels and evidence requirements. The calibration round asks whether a selected excerpt supports a named code, with the full ad available as context. A negative excerpt decision does not imply that the entire ad lacks that competency. Earlier reviewer feedback was preserved.
  7. Marketing extension. Marketing was scoped to strategy, customer experience, channel/partner work and transformation, rather than all advertising, sales or communications. Its 500-ad review and 20-category map are retained as an earlier AI-assisted full-ad interpretation.
  8. Software engineering and public release. A further 1,000 software-engineering ads were selected from the same pinned source. The 59 UXR/design/PM competencies were screened across their retained full texts; contextual review records positive, negative and uncertain evidence. A common automated AI screen is applied to all five occupations. The public release makes these different review states explicit.

Sources and sampling

The expanded source is OpenJobData, hosted by scholarweave, revision d1c2b9ef39a1090caeec3868183ef578cf24a21a. The retained source comprises 267 active/closed full partitions, supplemented by selected change records. Source files, provider fields, hashes, dates and canonical identifiers remain in the private research archive. The public download includes cohort metadata and hashes, not full descriptions.

Provider-specific recovery joins public job-description components in documented order, avoiding exact duplicate components. Source components and derived text hashes are retained. Text length is a screening aid, not proof of completeness. Native requisition IDs, matching URLs and conservative mirror evidence determine deduplication; superficially similar jobs are not automatically collapsed.

Occupations are retrieved using title and, where specified, department signals. Eligibility then considers the actual work, US role location, source date and recoverable text. No AI term, desired AI percentage, employer quota or sector quota determines selection. Generic titles can be ambiguous: software screening explicitly distinguishes software development from CNC programming, hardware installation, technical writing and other non-software meanings.

For UXR, design and PM, canonical candidates were ordered by the SHA256 digest of 20261016-openjob500-v1|role|posting_id, with stable-ID tie-breaking. Screening followed that fixed order until 500 eligible ads or frame exhaustion. The UXR frame produced 346 retained ads, so it was not padded to 500. Marketing uses seed 20261017-marketing500-v1 with its own frozen candidate queue and screening manifest. Software engineering uses seed 20261026-swe1000-v1 and a fixed hash-ordered queue; unresolved cases are excluded from the selected cohort and documented. Selection is random ordering within a retrieved and screened source frame, not a probability sample of all US vacancies.

Category development

The non-engineering categories are a hybrid deductive and inductive framework. Occupational practices, practitioner terminology, prior scholarship and user-proposed frontier activities supplied starting concepts. Job-ad evidence tested and refined their boundaries. This is not an independently established consensus taxonomy. A category may be retained because it is theoretically useful even when no supporting ad is detected.

Each competency has a stable identifier, definition, minimum evidence, inclusion and exclusion rules. Broader shared families connect activities across occupations without assuming that a similarly named task has the same meaning in every profession. The software-engineering labels and hierarchy are credited to Ng; study-authored rules translate those labels into observable ad evidence.

The intended schema separates: the competency; AI used in the worker’s own activity; AI as the object of product work; enabling other people; duty versus required, preferred, alternative or aspirational qualification; knowledge versus use, contribution, construction or leadership; and explicitly stated initiative stage. Employer branding and AI recruitment policies are context. Current automated outputs do not reliably populate every intended schema field.

Coding and units of analysis

The source unit is a complete recovered ad. The review unit is a selected passage interpreted with directly relevant full-ad context. The counting unit is one ad per competency, regardless of repeated mentions. Multiple different competencies can be counted in one ad. Knowledge-only requirements and alternative qualifications are not silently converted into task-performance obligations.

Automated retrieval uses explicit AI vocabulary and activity patterns to identify candidate passages. A common detector is applied identically to all five cohorts for the comparison table. It is deliberately treated as a text screen: it can still mistake a company capability for an employee expectation. It has not been validated as a measure of occupational AI demand.

Contextual AI review accepts, rejects or marks a candidate unclear under the operational rule. Review can add a missed code when the retained evidence supports it. Evidence offsets and text hashes allow exact-span checks. The audit has already rejected company-only agent claims, ordinary research methods mistaken for AI methods, and “orchestration” of human teams mistaken for agent orchestration. It has also found missed agent-oversight duties in full context. Exact-span checks verify quotation provenance; they do not validate the interpretation.

The maps deliberately distinguish automated candidate counts from earlier AI-reviewed marketing detections. Supplemental reviewed counts represent supported evidence in the reviewed subset, not completed full-corpus prevalence. Ads with no candidate match still require a recall audit. The public examples are illustrative and selected for interpretability; they are not an accuracy sample. All 696 original candidate-ad packets across UXR, design and PM have now received contextual AI review; additional diagnostic reads repaired specific misses. This still leaves unmatched ads and unexamined passages without exhaustive review. Independent human coding, inter-rater reliability and classifier precision/recall are not reported because those validation steps have not been completed.

Interpreting differences

Differences between occupations can reflect actual advertised expectations, occupational scope, seniority, employer composition, geography, source coverage, text recovery or coding. The broad pilot rates, stricter audit rates, frontier competency detections and new common-screen rates are separate measures. They must not be spliced into a single ranking or time series.

The most defensible current conclusions concern what particular ads explicitly ask for and which candidate activities warrant further study. Apparent rarity requires careful reporting: use the number of reviewed supported ads, the denominator, candidate-review coverage, and retrieval/validation limits together. “Not detected” does not mean “does not exist.”

Reproducibility and preservation

The public repository includes the frontend, public aggregates, codebook, selected source metadata, capture utility, release builder and quality checks. Complete descriptions, raw provider payloads, prior calibration feedback and private working files remain outside the publication. Existing local pilots and feedback have not been overwritten. Rebuilding the release requires the documented retained study inputs; the public site alone is not a full raw-data reproduction package.

A separate October 26 collection tests prospective monitoring. Its candidate ads are not appended to the analyzed baseline. The source manifest reports closure events that were absent from the listed full-change files; the capture records this discrepancy. No complete change-cohort or trend claim is made from that batch.
