EXPLORATORY RELEASE · OCTOBER 2026

Monitoring and taxonomy protocol

This is a proposed research protocol with working collection infrastructure. The public baseline is available; automated monthly publication, an expert panel and independent human validation have not been activated or completed.

Keep two layers

Freeze a small set of headline measures and stable competency identifiers for a defined reporting year. Alongside them, maintain a discovery layer for unfamiliar activities, new tools and changed task boundaries. A new tool name does not automatically justify a new category. A new task, object of work, responsibility or required judgment may.

Stable measures support comparison. The discovery layer prevents the taxonomy from missing change simply because old definitions lack the language for it. The dashboard should show when a category was introduced and whether its earlier counts were back-coded or are unavailable.

Build categories systematically

  1. Anchor occupational scope. Crosswalk each profession to a documented task framework such as O*NET or ESCO, recognizing that emerging HCI and marketing specialties may not align cleanly. Use those frameworks as scope anchors, not evidence that an AI activity is prevalent.
  2. Recruit a bounded panel. A proposed panel of 5–8 practitioners per occupation should vary in seniority, organization size, industry and AI experience. Include ordinary adopters and skeptics alongside frontier practitioners. Record conflicts and panel composition.
  3. Separate discovery from measurement. Use purposively varied ads and expert accounts to discover categories. Freeze definitions before coding a separate probability-ordered source cohort for frequencies. Do not enrich the prevalence sample with AI-heavy examples.
  4. Run two structured rounds. Ask panelists independently to identify missing activities, sort examples, flag ambiguous boundaries and rate whether a category is distinct, useful and observable in an ad. Discuss disagreements, revise once, then retain unresolved alternatives rather than manufacture consensus.
  5. Document every decision. Record which evidence motivated a category, what was merged or split, the date/version, and the effect on historical comparability. Treat panel judgments as content-validity evidence, not workforce prevalence.

Specify the denominator before each collection

The proposed primary monthly cohort is unique, eligible US postings whose original posting date falls in that calendar month and whose description is captured within a fixed grace period. Independently retain first-seen cohorts and active-stock snapshots as secondary measures. Ads with missing or contradictory original dates form a separate stratum. Inserts may be collector backfills; updates may be wording changes; closures are not new ads.

Use the same provider coverage and retrieval rules each month where possible. Record source outages, added employers and schema changes. Report a constant-source panel alongside the expanding source frame. Count a vacancy once per monthly cohort using conservative requisition identity, with reposting rules fixed in advance.

Freeze the text version used for the cohort and keep later versions as revisions. Using today’s description with an old posting date cannot reconstruct historical wording. The existing 2026 snapshot therefore supports recency sensitivity, not a retrospective monthly trend.

Validate before treating counts as prevalence

After shared calibration, independently double-code a probability sample stratified by occupation, candidate status and category family. Include randomly drawn ads with no automated matches. Separately oversample rare candidate activities to understand their precision; retain the sampling probabilities. This separates a prevalence sample from diagnostic examples.

Predefine code-level decisions and resolve disagreements only after saving independent judgments. Report agreement and precision/recall with uncertainty and sample sizes. Sparse categories may need qualitative reporting and pooled periods rather than unstable monthly percentages. An error-corrected estimate requires design weights and uncertainty propagation; do not substitute the proportion of accepted purposive examples.

For each competency publish: supported ads divided by all eligible ads; stricter execution-level expectations; employer reach; known-date-only and corroborated-US sensitivity; candidate and review coverage; and uncertainty appropriate to the source sample. Occupation-wide inference remains limited by source coverage. Do not report a national adoption rate from these ads.

Handle category changes without breaking the series

  • Editorial wording changes keep the stable ID and record a minor version when meaning is unchanged.
  • New detection vocabulary requires a detector version and replay on retained historical text, with side-by-side old and new counts.
  • A changed inclusion rule, split or merge requires a major taxonomy version. Bridge-code an overlap sample under both versions and, where feasible, recode the full retained history.
  • When back-coding is not possible, show a visible series break. Do not join incompatible counts with a continuous line.
  • Retired categories remain in archived releases. Discovery cases do not acquire historical zeros simply because they were not previously measured.

Working collection utility

scripts/capture_source.py pins a source revision, saves the manifest, verifies file sizes and SHA256 hashes, retains title-retrieved raw candidate payloads locally, and reconciles event counts. It is restartable and has a download-size bound. It does not perform occupational eligibility or publish new estimates.

Run collection outside the public repository, retain the raw archive securely, and complete recovery, screening, deduplication, coding, validation and release checks before updating the dashboard. The first fresh test retained 7,697 candidate versions across the five retrieval routes, with overlapping role tags. That number is neither a US cohort nor a count of newly posted vacancies.

Distinct contribution to test

The potential contribution is a traceable account of changing occupational activities: who is expected to use AI, who shapes AI products, who builds tools for colleagues, and what forms of judgment and oversight appear. A versioned framework linking these activities across five professions could complement broader economy-wide keyword or exposure measures. Its value depends on transparent denominators, independent validation, expert input and repeated comparable observations, not the number of categories alone.
